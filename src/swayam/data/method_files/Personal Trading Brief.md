### Core system-design principle — the AI is an ELIMINATOR, not a RECOMMENDER

The AI's primary job is to **REMOVE trades from consideration, not to add them.** Workflow:

1. All possible NIFTY setups today (say 10 candidates).
2. AI applies your rules → **eliminates the 6-7** that fail regime, R:R, event risk, operational readiness, etc.
3. What remains (2-3 setups) → **your judgment call.** You pick, or you skip everything.
4. If you take a trade → full journal discipline kicks in.

**Why this design:** it preserves your pattern-recognition and chart-reading skill (which is real — 4 years' worth), while removing the failure mode where you took a trade because you *felt* like trading (overtrading) instead of because it met your criteria. The system polices the discipline; you keep the craft.

**In your own words (2026-09-03):** *"I want this system to tell me not to take these trades, meaning the system will tell me what all to avoid. If I have 10 options to take a trade and it removes 6 or 7 out of them, then I will have 2 or 3 options, and that will be my judgment call after that."*

## Risk management — Phase 1 rules

### Fixed (non-negotiable)

- **Margin base:** ~₹8–9 lakh, combining **cash margin + pledged-share margin**. The 1% cap applies to the total.
- **Maximum risk per single trade: 1% of margin base.** Hard cap. Roughly ₹8,000–9,000 per trade. Not "1% for normal, 2% for great setups." **1%. Every trade.**
- **Stop-loss level derives from that number**, not from chart aesthetics. You place the stop where the setup invalidates, then size the position so a stop-out costs exactly 1% — no more.
- **Order type: 99% limit orders.** Market orders permitted only for hedging cheap options (₹5–10 premium range) where NIFTY's high liquidity makes slippage negligible. Never for entry or exit of a directional trade.
- **Transaction-cost floor rule (added 2026-09-03, from FYERS audit).** No trade whose expected gross P&L is too small to overcome round-trip charges. FY25-26 evidence: 246 scalping contracts generated ₹92,408 in charges that fully consumed a +₹6,109 gross profit. Rules must think in **NET** terms — every expected-value calculation subtracts realistic per-contract charges before deciding whether the trade is worth taking.
